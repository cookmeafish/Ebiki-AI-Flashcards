## d2-extras last card, 2.4 "Dictionary, rainbow table and credential stuffing attacks"
- Pass 1 change 1 (one source or many) kept, but the line also claimed "a higher than normal success rate". Neither
  MITRE nor OWASP backs that: MITRE T1110.004 lists bursts of failed logins with rotating usernames from one source;
  the OWASP prevention cheat sheet names logins to multiple accounts from one address and low, steady traffic spread
  over proxies and many addresses, and says nothing about success rates. Now: "Indicator of stuffing: a burst of logins
  to many different accounts, each tried once or twice, from one address or spread thin over many (botnets, proxies)".
  Read: https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1110/004/ and
  https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Credential_Stuffing_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html (added to sources).
- Pass 1 change 2 (OWASP URL) kept: the page loads; "automated injection of stolen username and password pairs", MFA
  "a primary counter-measure". Read: https://community.owasp.org/attacks/Credential_stuffing
- Pass 1 change 3 kept: salting "protects against an attacker's pre-computing hashes using rainbow tables", work
  factor makes each hash more expensive. Read:
  https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html . T1110/002 (rainbow table
  mention) was not re-fetched.
- NIST source updated from SP 800-63-3 to SP 800-63-4: https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html . Read there
  (section 3.1.1.2): verifiers SHALL compare a new password against a blocklist of commonly used, expected or
  compromised passwords (examples include breach corpuses and dictionary words), and passwords SHALL be salted and
  hashed with a suitable password hashing scheme, cost factor as high as practical. The card's rules still hold.
- No change: brute force vs dictionary, offline rainbow tables vs unique salts, stuffing vs spraying, defenses.
