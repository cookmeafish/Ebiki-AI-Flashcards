## d4a-extras last card, 4.5 "Email encryption: S/MIME and PGP"
1. Key point 1: PGP and S/MIME encrypt a one time session key with the recipient's public key, not the whole message
   (RFC 4880 section 2.1). Added "(it locks a one time message key)" so the line is exact.
   Source: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4880
2. Key point 3 said SPF, DKIM and DMARC "check which servers and domains may send the mail". DKIM is a domain
   signature, not a sender list; NIST SP 800-177r1 groups all three as sending domain authentication. Now: "check that
   mail really comes from the domain it claims; they do not hide the content".
   Source: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/177/r1/final
3. Added https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9580 to sources: it obsoletes RFC 4880 (read in its abstract). RFC 4880
   kept since the card's facts were checked there too.
- Checked and kept: S/MIME signatures give authentication, integrity and non-repudiation, encryption gives
  confidentiality (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8551 abstract); OpenPGP trust through introducers and trust
  signatures (web of trust; RFC 4880 section 5.2.3.13); TLS protects transmission only (NIST SP 800-177r1).
